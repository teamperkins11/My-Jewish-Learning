Friday October 23, 2026 · Genesis 12:1–17:27

From Noah's righteousness to Abraham's courage

Last week we studied Noah, called righteous because he resisted the corruption around him. Yet the Torah never records him pleading for his neighbors. Jewish interpreters contrast this with Abraham, who later pleads for the people of Sodom. This is a question raised by interpretation, not proof that Noah never spoke up.

Personal goodness means refusing wrongdoing and caring for those near us. Moral courage sometimes asks more: challenging unfairness, supporting someone excluded, or taking a principled risk. Abraham is called to leave the familiar and become a blessing beyond his household.

Torah: Genesis 12:1–3

Go forth from your land, your birthplace, and your father's house to the land that I will show you. ... And you shall be a blessing.

Rabbi Sacks invites us to consider the journey into uncertainty and the resilience required to keep moving toward a moral future.

Discuss at dinner

  1. When is refusing to participate in wrongdoing not enough?
  2. What is one change you made without knowing where it would lead?
  3. What values from our families should we preserve, and what assumptions should we question?
  4. How do the experiences of Sarai and Hagar complicate the story of our ancestors?
  5. How can our family be a blessing to people outside our home?

Closing reflection

May our home be a place of peace, and may that peace move outward. May we preserve what is good, have courage to question what is wrong, and become a blessing wherever our journey leads. Shabbat Shalom.
