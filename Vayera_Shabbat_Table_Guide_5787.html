Vayera — Shabbat Table Guide

Friday, October 30, 2026 · Genesis 18:1–22:24

Theme: Righteousness that acts: hospitality, justice, and the courage to question.

Opening reflection

In Noach we considered the limits of private righteousness. In Lech Lecha, Abraham answered a call to move beyond what he knew. In Vayera, Abraham welcomes strangers and argues with God over the fate of Sodom. Faith now becomes action on behalf of others.

Torah text: Genesis 18:25

הֲשֹׁפֵט כָּל־הָאָרֶץ לֹא יַעֲשֶׂה מִשְׁפָּט

Ha-shofet kol ha-aretz lo ya'aseh mishpat?

Shall the Judge of all the earth not do justice?

Abraham does not claim that Sodom is innocent. He asks whether the innocent may be destroyed with the guilty. This is moral courage directed toward justice rather than merely self-protection.

Beyond the heroic portrait

Vayera also recounts Sarah's suffering and laughter, Hagar and Ishmael's expulsion, and the binding of Isaac. The Torah does not flatten its protagonists into flawless heroes. Jewish interpretation asks us to attend to those with less power as well as to Abraham's courage.

Questions for dinner

  1. What is the difference between being a good person and defending someone else?
  2. Abraham asks God to act justly. When is questioning authority a moral obligation?
  3. What does hospitality require beyond inviting people into our home?
  4. How does Hagar's story challenge our assumptions about the heroes of Genesis?
  5. When should faith mean obedience, and when should it mean asking difficult questions? Consider the binding of Isaac.

Closing

May we build a home that welcomes others, a conscience that notices injustice, and a faith strong enough to ask difficult questions. Shabbat Shalom.
